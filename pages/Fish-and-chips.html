Fish and Chips
Fish & Chips is a timeless icon of British pub cuisine, celebrated far beyond its borders. Tender, translucent fillets of cod, enveloped in a delicate, golden beer batter, are paired with hand-cut potatoes, fried to a crisp exterior and a soft, fluffy heart. Simple in concept, exacting in execution, it is a pairing of perfect balance: texture, temperature and seasoning in harmony.
INGRIDIENS
  • 3 pounds Russet potatoes
  • 3 quarts canola oil, for frying
  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup cornstarch
  • 1/2 teaspoon cayenne pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon paprika
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground black pepper
  • 3 teaspoons Diamond kosher salt
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1 1/2 to 2 pounds cod fillet, 1 inch thick
  • Up to 3 cups beer
~ The Method ~
  1. Slice the fish into your preferred portion sizes. I like smaller pieces, about 2 ounces each, but sometimes you need to follow the natural cuts of the fillet. Try to keep the portions as evenly sized as possible. Pat dry with a paper towel and set aside in the refrigerator.
  2. Peel the potatoes and slice off the "top and tail" to square off the ends; discard the trimmings. Cut each potato lengthwise into 1/2-inch fries. Soak the potato pieces in a bowl of water for about 30 minutes, then remove and pat dry with a paper towel.
  3. In a heavy-bottomed Dutch oven (about 7-quart capacity), heat two of the three quarts of oil over medium heat to 350°F. Pro tip: use an oil-safe probe thermometer to monitor and control the temperature for perfect results.
  4. Add the fries to the hot oil and increase the heat to high. Fry, stirring with a mesh spider or slotted metal spoon, until the potatoes turn light golden and just begin to brown at the corners, 6 to 8 minutes. Transfer the fries to paper towels to drain.
  5. In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, cornstarch, cayenne, paprika, pepper, and salt. Remove 1/2 cup of this flour mixture, place it in a casserole dish, and set it aside for dredging.
  6. Add the baking powder to the flour mixture remaining in the bowl and whisk to combine. Add 1.5 cups of beer and stir until just combined (the batter will be lumpy). Add the remaining beer as needed, 1 tablespoon at a time, whisking after each addition, until the batter falls from the whisk in a thin, steady stream and leaves a faint trail across the surface.
  7. With the fries done, increase the heat to medium-high, add the remaining quart of oil if necessary, and heat the oil to 375°F. Meanwhile, thoroughly dry the fish with paper towels.
  8. Dredge each piece of fish in the reserved flour mixture, shaking off the excess. Dip one piece at a time into the batter and let the excess run off, shaking gently. Return the battered fish to the casserole dish with the flour mixture and turn to coat both sides. (This is messy, but worth it!)
  9. If you are working with a partner, add each piece directly to the hot oil. If you are working alone, keep the coated pieces in a single layer on a baking sheet and repeat until all the pieces are coated, working in batches and being careful not to let them sit too long, so the batter does not stick to the sheet pan. It is best to have one person frying and another dredging.
  10. As you add the fish, adjust the heat to keep the oil between 375°F and 385°F. Failing to maintain the oil temperature is a common and costly mistake: if it is too low, the fish will not crisp up, and if it is too high, everything will burn. Use an oil-safe thermometer for best results.
  11. Fry the fish, stirring occasionally, until golden brown, about 5 minutes depending on thickness. Use a kitchen spider to remove the fish from the hot oil and transfer it to a sheet pan lined with a wire cooling rack. Never walk away from hot oil!
  12. Return the previously fried potatoes to the hot oil for about 2 minutes to crisp up. Remove them to a fresh paper towel or directly to a cooling rack, and immediately sprinkle with sea salt or kosher salt.
  13. Serve the fish and chips with traditional malt vinegar and tartar sauce.
